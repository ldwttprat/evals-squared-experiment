Based on the sources provided, here are the three most important themes regarding AI evaluations (evals) and a one-sentence recommendation for each:
1. Grounding Evals in Real Data and Error AnalysisBefore jumping straight into writing automated tests, it is essential to look directly at your AI application's data and user interaction traces to understand what is actually going wrong 1-3. Doing basic counting and categorization of these actual errors prevents developers from wasting time on hypothetical problems 4, 5.
* Recommendation: Systematically manually review and categorize real user logs to identify the most prevalent failure modes before writing any automated evaluation tests 3, 6, 7.
2. Deploying LLMs as Automated JudgesWhen dealing with complex failure modes that are difficult to evaluate with standard code, developers can use LLMs as judges to automatically check for specific errors 8, 9. However, you must test the LLM judge's performance against your own manual reviews to ensure it actually aligns with human expectations 10, 11.
* Recommendation: Design LLM judges to answer tightly scoped, binary (pass/fail) questions about specific errors, and always mathematically verify that their judgments align with human grading before relying on them 12-14.
3. The Crucial Role of Human Domain ExpertsDespite the advanced capabilities of AI, human context is fundamentally required to accurately identify "bad product smells," correct hallucinations, and establish the standard for quality 15-17. Rather than relying on a slow committee, organizations benefit from having a single trusted expert manage this process 18.
* Recommendation: Appoint a "benevolent dictator"—such as a product manager or specific domain expert—to manually review traces, cut through organizational noise, and define the qualitative standards for your AI product 18, 19.
